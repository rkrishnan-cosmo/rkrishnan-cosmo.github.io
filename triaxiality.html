Triaxiality of galaxy clusters

Galaxy clusters are elongated in three dimensions, but most weak-lensing analyses model them as spheres. This project measures their shape directly from the lensing signal.

LSST DESC Galaxy Cluster Working Group, Fall 2023 to present. Paper in internal review (2025).

Up to 20%weak-lensing mass bias when a triaxial halo is projected along the line of sight (Herbonnet et al. 2019)
Monopole + quadrupolefit simultaneously for mass, concentration and ellipticity
DES Y3 redMaPPerclusters stacked along their major axis

Why it matters

Rubin LSST will measure cluster lensing at much higher signal-to-noise than earlier surveys, so the spherical-NFW assumption will become a leading systematic. Modeling the elliptical extension, and the quadrupole lensing signal it produces, lets us correct for it instead of averaging over it.

Approach

  • Software. I developed a module inside CLMM v2, the Cluster Lens Mass Modeling library of LSST DESC, that fits the projected 2-D ellipticity in the plane of the sky, following Shin et al. (2018).
  • Simulations. Tested on elliptical-NFW shear mock catalogs and on SkySim5000, where ellipticity and mass are recovered accurately.
  • Data. Applied to DES Y3 redMaPPer clusters. The major axis of each cluster is computed from its satellite galaxy distribution using second moments, and clusters are aligned along it before stacking.
  • Cross-check. Consistent with the ellipticity and mass reported by McClintock et al. (2019) for DES Y1 redMaPPer clusters.

Paper

“Constraining Galaxy Cluster Triaxiality via Weak Lensing – I. Preparation for the Rubin Data Beyond Leading Order”, R. Srinivasan, S. Fu, R. Solomon, T. Shin, et al. (2025). In internal review in the LSST DESC collaboration.
